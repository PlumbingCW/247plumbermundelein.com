How to Keep Drains From Clogging: A Mundelein, IL Homeowner Guide

Most clogs build slowly from grease, hair, wipes and soap film. A few habits and a yearly check keep them from turning into a backup.

Call (847) 281-8626

By the Mundelein 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026. Updated November 12, 2026.

Know what each drain can handle

Kitchen drains see grease, starch and food scraps. Bathroom drains see hair, soap and toothpaste. Toilets are built for human waste and toilet paper, and nothing else. When a drain receives something it was not designed for, the clog starts as a thin layer on the pipe wall and grows from there.

Think of the pipe as a narrow road. A few small obstacles do not stop traffic, but they add up. Most blockages are weeks or months in the making.

Kitchen habits that matter most

Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing. Pour cooled cooking fat into a can and throw it out. Hot water does not fix grease, because it cools farther down the line and sticks to the wall of the pipe. A garbage disposal does not make this safe either.

Run cold water for a few seconds after using the disposal. Avoid coffee grounds, eggshells, rice, pasta and fibrous scraps like celery or corn husks. They pack into a plug that a disposal motor cannot clear.

Bathroom habits

A cheap hair catcher in the shower is the single best investment. Hair wraps around soap scum and forms a net that catches everything else. In the sink, a small stopper basket does the same job.

Do not flush wipes, even if the package says flushable. They do not break down like paper and they snag on rough pipe joints. Cotton swabs, dental floss and feminine products belong in the trash.

Why chemical cleaners are a poor habit

Liquid drain openers can eat through a clog, but they also sit in the pipe and attack old metal joints, rubber seals and plastic fittings. If they do not clear the clog, a plumber now has to work around a pool of harsh chemical. That is a safety issue for the person with the cable.

A plunger, a hand drain snake or a bit of hot water with dish soap are better first steps. If those do not work, call. It costs less than damage to a pipe.

Warning signs of a deeper problem

One slow sink is normal. Several slow drains at once points to the main line. Gurgling toilets, water in a basement floor drain after laundry and a sewer smell are all signs the main is partly blocked. These need a professional, not another bottle of cleaner.

Tree roots are a common cause in older lots. A camera inspection shows exactly what is in the line and how long it has been developing.

How grease really moves through a pipe

When hot, greasy water enters a cold pipe it cools and clings to the wall as a soft film. The next load adds another layer, and starch and soap bind it into a hardened collar. Over months that collar narrows the pipe until even a small amount of paste or hair can close it.

That is why a drain can be fine for years and then stop in a week. The flow area was shrinking all along. A yearly jetting or cable cleaning resets the pipe to full width.

Floor drains and laundry lines

Basement floor drains sit unused for months, and their trap seals can dry out and let sewer gas in. Pour a quart of water in every few months. Laundry lines carry lint and detergent that bond into a thick sludge, so a lint trap on the washer discharge helps.

If the standpipe overflows during a wash cycle, the line is partly blocked. Do not keep running loads. Call.

Codes and standards

The drain, waste and vent rules in the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code) set pipe slope and trap design, which is why a drain that was installed correctly rarely clogs without a cause.

What to do first when a drain slows

Start with the safest steps.

  • Use a plunger or a hand snake before any chemical product.
  • If you do use a chemical cleaner, never mix it with another and keep the room ventilated.
  • Stop using the fixture if water rises or if several drains slow at once.
  • Call a plumber when a gurgle or a smell comes with the slow drain.

Mundelein notes for older and newer lots

Houses near downtown Mundelein and the Metra station tend to be older and may still have clay or cast iron laterals that roots and scale can narrow. Newer subdivisions along Route 60 and Route 83 have plastic laterals that are smooth, but low spots and grease still cause trouble.

For water main or street-side questions in the village, Mundelein Public Works is at 847-949-3270. Anything inside your property line is yours to deal with, and a camera tells you where the problem sits.

A simple yearly drain routine

  • Clean pop-up stoppers and shower strainers each month.
  • Flush a kettle of hot water and a squirt of dish soap down the kitchen drain every few weeks.
  • Run water in seldom-used basement floor drains so the trap seal stays full.
  • Have the main line cameraed if you have repeat slow drains or old clay pipe.
  • Know where your cleanout is and keep access clear.

When to get help

If a plunger does not clear a drain after a few tries, stop. Repeated plunging in a partly blocked line can push water into places you do not want it.
