Basement waterproofing in Mundelein, IL starts with finding the source

Basement waterproofing is the set of steps that keep groundwater out of a below-grade space. For a plumbing company, that means checking whether the water comes from a pipe, a drain, a sump pit or the soil outside. Each has a different fix.

We take a practical approach. If a pipe is leaking, we repair it. If groundwater is the issue, we install or improve the drainage system that carries water to a sump pit and out of the house. For structural cracks or exterior regrading, we say so and point you to the right trade.

Where basement water comes from

  • A leaking supply or drain pipe, which a plumber can locate.
  • A floor drain that backs up when the sewer or storm flow is high.
  • Groundwater rising through the floor slab at the cove joint.
  • Water entering through a wall crack or a window well.
  • Surface water from poor grading or a downspout that discharges at the foundation.

What plumbers do and do not do

Plumbers handle pipes, drains, sump pits and pumps. We do not rebuild foundation walls or re-grade a yard. If your wet basement needs exterior excavation or structural repair, we tell you plainly and recommend you gather quotes from a specialist. A plumbing repair is still a smart first step because it rules out a pipe leak.

Local factors in Mundelein basements

Parts of Mundelein sit near lakes, including Diamond Lake and Countryside Lake. Lots close to a lake or a low area can see the water table rise after spring thaw or a big storm. A sump pit and a working pump are the most common answer, with a battery backup for outages. See sump pump repair for pump options.

Floor drains connect to the sewer, so a heavy storm that stresses the sewer can push water through them. A backwater valve can help in some homes. Village Public Works at 847-949-3270 can tell you whether storm flow is affecting your street. For clogged floor drains, see drain cleaning.

What to note before the visit

Write down where water appears, how long after rain it shows up and whether it dries on its own. Photograph the wall, the floor joint and the sump pit. Note any recent changes outside, such as new landscaping or a changed downspout.

These details save time because they separate groundwater from a plumbing leak. They also keep a quote honest, since the repair should match how the water actually enters.

Codes and permits for drainage work

Interior drain tile and sump work involve piping covered by the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), and structural or exterior work can need village permits. Ask the Mundelein building office at 847-949-3283 before work starts.

What to do before we arrive at a flooding basement

Safety comes before cleanup when water is standing in a basement.

  • Stay out of standing water if outlets, appliances or the panel are nearby, since water and electricity can kill.
  • Switch off power to the basement from a dry panel, or call the utility if the panel is wet.
  • Move stored items up off the floor and photograph the water line.
  • Do not run the washer or dishwasher, which add water to the drain.
  • Call (847) 281-8626 and describe where the water enters.
