Which water and sewer system do you have?

The village says parts of Hawthorn Woods have municipal water or sewer, two deep municipal wells operated through Aqua Illinois serve a portion of the village, and individual wells may be permitted by the Lake County Health Department where municipal water is not extended. Areas without sewer use septic systems permitted by the same department.

That is why the first question from our 24/7 dispatcher is about your system. A well house with a pressure tank fails differently from a house on village water, and a septic tank backup is not the same as a sewer lateral problem. Estate-size homes and hot water

Large homes often use two water heaters or a large tank with a recirculation loop to keep hot water close to distant bathrooms. When one unit fails, the whole house notices. We size replacements to the actual demand and check the recirculation pump and controls at the same visit.

If a heater is installed in a finished room, we plan for a drain pan and leak sensor so a future failure causes less damage.

Winter on well and septic properties

A well house can freeze if the heater fails, and a frozen pressure tank or line leaves the house without water. Keep the well house heated and insulated, and know where the pump breaker is. Septic lines run closer to the surface than many people think, so avoid driving over the tank.

If the water stops in winter, call. We check the pump, switch and lines and tell you what part is at fault.

Rules for well and septic homes

House plumbing follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code). Private wells and septic systems are permitted by the Lake County Health Department, so we separate the plumbing work from the system work and tell you which office handles each.
