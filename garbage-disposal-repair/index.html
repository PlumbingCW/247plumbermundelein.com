Garbage disposal repair in Mundelein, IL

A garbage disposal grinds food waste so it can pass through the drain. When it jams, hums without spinning, goes silent or leaks into the cabinet, the kitchen sink usually stops working too. Disposal repair is a mix of electrical reset, mechanical unjamming and leak tracing.

Our 24/7 line sends a licensed plumber with disposal parts and a plan. If the unit is old or the motor has failed, we explain the replacement and install a new one.

Disposal jammed or leaking: do this first

Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it is off. Turn off the wall switch and unplug it, or switch off the breaker, before looking. If water is leaking from the bottom of the unit, turn off the supply under the sink and place a container under it. Then call the 24/7 line.

Problems we see

  • A unit that hums but does not turn, which is a jam.
  • A unit that is silent, which may be a tripped reset button or a failed motor.
  • Leaks from the sink flange, the discharge pipe or the body of the disposal.
  • A kitchen sink that drains slowly even after the disposal is cleared.
  • A dishwasher that backs up into the sink because the disposal knockout plug is blocked.
  • Bad smells that return after cleaning.

When the disposal and the drain are linked

A disposal drains into the same trap and line as the sink. If water backs up when the disposal runs, the clog may sit past the unit. Our drain cleaning service clears the line. If the dishwasher also drains into the disposal, we check the hose connection and the knockout plug.

For installs, disposals need a nearby electrical outlet or hardwire and a drain connection. We also review the sink and cabinet clearance. If the faucet leaks too, we handle faucet repair during the same visit.

What affects disposal prices

Repairs are modest while installs depend on the unit you choose.

  • Unit power. Higher horsepower units cost more.
  • Mounting and wiring. A hardwired unit takes longer than a plug-in.
  • Dishwasher hookup. The connection is reconnected or replaced.

What should never go in a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds, fibrous peels, pasta, rice and bones are the usual causes of jams and slow drains. Even with a disposal, scraps belong in the trash or compost when they are bulky. Run cold water before, during and after each use.

Following those rules costs nothing and often doubles the life of the unit. If your disposal keeps jamming, the cause may be the motor size or the drain behind it.

Rules for disposal work

Disposal work touches the drain and the electrical supply. The drain side follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), and a new circuit follows the electrical code. The Mundelein building office at 847-949-3283 can say whether wiring needs a permit.

What to do before we arrive at a jammed disposal

Keep your hands out of the unit.

  • Turn the wall switch off, and unplug the unit or flip its breaker before touching anything.
  • Never put fingers in the disposal, even when it looks stopped.
  • Use the hex key socket under the unit, or a wooden spoon handle from above, to free a jam.
  • Press the red reset button once the unit has cooled.
  • Do not pour drain cleaner into a jammed disposal.
