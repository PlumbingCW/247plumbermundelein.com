Water heater repair when the tank stops working

Emergency water heater repair means finding out why a heater has stopped heating, started leaking or begun making noise, then fixing the part that failed. Most calls in Mundelein fall into three groups. The burner or element is not firing, the tank is leaking, or the water is lukewarm and runs out quickly.

A licensed plumber arrives with a tester, common igniters, thermocouples, elements and valves. Many repairs finish in one visit. When the tank itself is failing, we say so plainly and explain why replacement costs less than another repair.

When a water heater problem is an emergency

Treat these as urgent and call the 24/7 line.

  • Water pooling around the base of the tank, which usually means the tank wall has failed.
  • A gas smell near the heater. Leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call us.
  • A relief valve that discharges hot water or steam repeatedly.
  • Rust-colored hot water together with a metallic smell or a damp spot on the floor.
  • A popping or rumbling sound from the tank that gets louder each week.

What to do while you wait

If water is leaking, shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater and turn off the power at the breaker, or set a gas heater to the off position. Place a bucket under any drip and move stored items off the floor. In a basement, keep out of standing water until the power to that area is off.

Do not touch the relief valve discharge pipe, because the water can be scalding. We handle both burst pipe repair and heater leaks, so mention if a supply line is also spraying.

Gas, electric and no-hot-water diagnosis

On a gas heater the first checks are the pilot or igniter, the thermocouple and the gas control valve. A pilot that will not stay lit often points to a worn thermocouple. A clogged burner tube or a blocked flue draws different symptoms, such as soot or a weak flame.

On an electric heater the usual cause of no hot water is a failed upper or lower element or a tripped high-limit switch. We test each element with a meter before replacing anything. If only the upper half of the tank is cold or only the lower, that tells us which element failed.

Lukewarm water that runs out fast often means sediment has built up on the bottom of the tank or a dip tube has broken. Flushing helps with sediment, and Lake Michigan water supplied to Mundelein carries moderate hardness, so scale builds up gradually over the years.

What drives the repair price

The price depends on the part and the access. You hear the number before we start.

  • Part needed. An igniter, element or valve sits in a different price band than a gas control.
  • Heater type. Tankless units have more electronics and need specialized parts.
  • Location. A heater in a tight utility closet takes longer than one in an open basement.
  • Hour of the call. After-hours dispatch is priced separately from a weekday visit.
  • Venting and code. A failed vent or missing expansion tank may need correcting while we are there.

Many manufacturers carry parts warranties, so keep the model and serial number from the data plate handy when you call.

Hot water in a home with Lake Michigan supply

Mundelein buys treated Lake Michigan water through the Central Lake County Joint Action Water Agency, which reports hardness of about 8 grains per gallon. That is moderate, so heaters in this area scale over time but not as quickly as in well-water towns. A yearly flush of the tank and a check of the anode rod extend life.

If you are comparing options after a failed heater, our water heater installation page covers sizing and venting, and the tankless water heater page explains when on-demand heat makes sense for a Mundelein home.

Standards for heater repairs

Repairs to a gas heater are judged against the manufacturer's instructions and the National Fuel Gas Code, NFPA 54, as a national benchmark for gas piping and venting. Water side work falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code). A relief valve must stay in place and piped to a safe discharge.
