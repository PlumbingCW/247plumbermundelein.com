Water softener and filtration in Mundelein, IL: a straight answer on hardness

The Central Lake County Joint Action Water Agency, which supplies Lake Michigan water to Mundelein, states that its water measures about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate. That is moderate hardness. Some households still choose a softener to reduce spots and scale on fixtures.

The agency also notes that most residents it spoke with stopped using softeners, and that softeners can make water more corrosive to plumbing. We share that so you decide with the full picture. If you still want a softener or a filter, we install and service them correctly.

Why people ask about softeners

  • White spots on glassware and fixtures.
  • Scale on faucet aerators and showerheads.
  • Dry skin or dull laundry that they associate with hard water.
  • A tankless water heater that needs frequent descaling.
  • A replacement for an older softener tank or valve.

Softener or filter: what each does

If you already have a softener

Check that the regeneration setting matches your actual hardness. Water at about 8 grains per gallon needs far less regeneration than a setting made for well water at 20 or more. Flush the system if you have been away for several days, and keep salt levels steady. We service valves, resin tanks and brine tanks. Many calls are valve replacements, not new systems.

Hardness in nearby towns differs

Not every nearby town gets the same water. Lake Zurich currently uses wells and the village reports that ion exchange treatment lowered hardness from 14 to 18 grains per gallon down to 6 to 8. Parts of Hawthorn Woods are on municipal wells operated through Aqua Illinois or on private wells. If you live outside Mundelein, check your own supplier before choosing a system. What affects the price

A softener has an installation cost and ongoing costs, and we explain both.

  • System type. Softener, filter or combination.
  • Install location. Distance to a floor drain and power.
  • Bypass and plumbing. Existing piping may need changes.
  • Running costs. Salt and water for regeneration.
  • Water heater. Hard water can shorten heater life, which a softener may help.

Salt, brine and the drain connection

A softener needs a drain for its regeneration cycle, an air gap that keeps wastewater from flowing back, an electrical outlet and a bypass valve. The brine tank needs periodic salt, and the resin bed eventually wears out.

Plan where the unit will sit. A cool, dry spot near the main shutoff and a floor drain makes installation simpler and service easier later. If you are on a septic system, ask about the discharge before buying.

Code for softener hookups

A softener drain needs an air gap, and the supply connections fall under the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code). Backflow protection and approved fittings protect the potable supply. If you are on a septic system, ask about brine discharge first.
