Sewer camera inspection in Mundelein, IL for a clear answer

A sewer camera inspection feeds a waterproof camera head through a cleanout and records the pipe as it goes. The plumber watches the picture live, notes the distance to each problem and locates it from above with a receiver. You receive a recording and a short written summary.

People call for three reasons. A line has backed up and they want to know why. They are buying a home and want to know what is underground. Or they have heard about roots and want proof before spending money.

What the camera can show

  • Root intrusion at joints and where it begins.
  • Cracks, collapsed sections and offset joints.
  • A belly or sag where water pools, shown by standing water in the picture.
  • Grease, scale or buildup that narrows the pipe.
  • The pipe material, such as clay, cast iron or PVC, and where it changes.

How an inspection runs

  1. 1
    Locate the accessWe find a cleanout or pull a toilet if none exists.
  2. 2
    Run the cameraThe head travels toward the house or the street while recording.
  3. 3
    Locate defectsA locator marks the surface above each problem and notes the depth.
  4. 4
    ExplainYou receive the recording and a plain summary of what to repair, watch or ignore.

Buying or selling a house with a sewer inspection

A home inspection often does not include the sewer lateral. A camera pass before closing can show a failing clay line or a root mat that the standard inspection never reaches. Sellers use the recording to answer questions up front and avoid surprises late in the sale.

Houses near downtown Mundelein and the Metra station are older than those in newer subdivisions along Route 60, and the camera is the fastest way to tell whether an older lateral is still sound. If the results show damage, our sewer line repair page covers the options.

What affects inspection pricing

Inspection prices are set by access and by how much of the line needs recording.

  • Access. A cleanout makes the job faster than a toilet pull.
  • Length. A long run to the street takes more time.
  • Locating. Marking the surface above each defect adds time.
  • Cleaning first. A line packed with debris needs clearing before the camera can see.
  • Report. A written summary and recording are included, and we explain the findings.

Combine an inspection with cleaning

If the camera cannot get past buildup, we clear the line first through drain cleaning or jetting, then record. That way you do not pay for a second visit. Where roots return again and again, a regular inspection schedule catches them before they stop the flow.

Inspections when you buy or sell

A sewer camera inspection is a smart addition to a home inspection, especially for older houses with clay or cast iron laterals. A recorded video gives the buyer a basis for negotiating and the seller a record that avoids surprises late in the sale.

What the camera report refers to

A camera report describes defects, and any repair it leads to falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code). Ask for the video file, not just a summary, so a second contractor can review the same footage.

When to call right away about a sewer line

A sewer problem can be a health hazard.

  • Sewage in a basement or a tub means stop using water and call at once.
  • Keep children and pets out of the area, and wear gloves and boots if you must enter.
  • Do not run the washer or dishwasher while a backup is active.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurance claim.
