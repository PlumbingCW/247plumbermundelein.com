Tankless water heater service in Mundelein, IL

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, using a heat exchanger instead of a storage tank. Service means installing the unit, diagnosing faults by error code, and flushing the heat exchanger so it can transfer heat properly.

Callers often tell us a previous company said they could replace the unit but not service it. We do both. A licensed plumber reads the code, checks gas supply, venting and flow, and gives you a repair-or-replace answer in plain terms.

Symptoms we see on tankless units

  • An error code on the display, or a unit that shuts off mid-shower.
  • Water that turns cold for a few seconds between hot bursts.
  • Hot water that takes a long time to arrive or never gets hot enough.
  • A rattling or kettle-like sound from the unit as scale builds.
  • A flow so low that the burner will not fire at all.

Flush and descale: the part owners skip

Minerals in the water collect on the heat exchanger and narrow the passages, which forces the unit to work harder and trips error codes. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the exchanger with a small pump, then rinses it out. We also clean the inlet screen and test combustion.

Mundelein water arrives from Lake Michigan through the Central Lake County Joint Action Water Agency at a hardness near 8 grains per gallon. That is moderate, but over years it still leaves scale, so most manufacturers call for an annual service. Skipping it is the most common reason we replace a unit early.

Gas lines and venting decide the install

Tankless units burn gas faster than tanks and often need a larger gas line than the one serving the old heater. They also use stainless or approved plastic vent pipe, not the old flue. We check both before quoting, which is why a site visit comes before a firm price. The water heater installation page explains the usual steps.

What affects tankless pricing

Whether you need service or an install, the price depends on these items.

  • Service versus install. A flush is a short visit while a new install involves gas, venting and water lines.
  • Model and fuel. Gas and electric tankless units differ in parts and labor.
  • Scale level. Heavy buildup takes more time and may mean replacing parts.
  • Gas line size. Larger piping adds cost but gives the unit what it needs.
  • Recirculation. A pump or return line adds comfort and adds work.

Recirculation pumps and long waits for hot water

Some Mundelein homeowners ask about a recirculation pump to cut the wait at distant fixtures. The pump moves hot water through a return loop or the cold line so hot water is waiting near the tap. It can add a small energy cost and needs a unit that supports it. We review the layout and tell you whether it makes sense for your home.

If your question is about an older tank, see water heater repair for the usual failure points.

Standards for tankless units

Tankless units are installed to the manufacturer's venting and gas sizing sheet, the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), and for gas models the NFPA 54 National Fuel Gas Code as a benchmark. A gas line sized for a tank heater often needs upgrading before a tankless unit can run at full rating.

Safety steps before a tankless service call

A gas unit with a fault should be treated with care.

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.
  • Turn the unit off at the control if it shows an ignition or venting error.
  • Shut off the cold supply to the unit if it is leaking.
  • Keep the vent and the air intake clear of snow and nests.
  • Call (847) 281-8626 and read the error code to the dispatcher.
