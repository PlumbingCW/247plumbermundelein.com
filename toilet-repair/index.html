Toilet repair in Mundelein, IL starts with stopping the water

Toilet repair covers clogs, leaks at the base, running tanks, weak flushes and rocking fixtures. An emergency usually means water on the floor, either from an overflow or a leak at the supply or wax ring.

Our 24/7 line sends a licensed plumber who first stops the water, then finds the cause. Most toilet faults are quick to fix. If the bowl is cracked or the toilet is decades old, we show you why replacement is wiser than another repair.

Toilet overflowing: do this now

Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float so the tank stops filling. Put towels around the base and open a window if the odor is strong. Do not flush again. Call us if the water does not stop or the bowl will not clear.

Common toilet problems

  • A clog that a plunger cannot clear, or a repeated slow flush.
  • A leak around the base, usually a failed wax ring or a loose flange.
  • A tank that runs constantly because of a worn flapper or fill valve.
  • Condensation or cracks in the tank that drip onto the floor.
  • A toilet that rocks, which can break the seal and leak below the floor.

When the toilet and the sewer are linked

If a toilet gurgles when you run a shower or if more than one fixture is slow, the clog is probably in the main line, not the bowl. A quick check with a cable tells us. If the main is blocked, our sewer line cleaning page explains the next step.

Lakeside lots with older plumbing can have vent problems that slow flushes. A plumber can check the vent stack when a toilet flushes poorly with no clog.

What affects toilet repair pricing

Most toilet repairs are quick, and the price is clear before we start.

  • Fault. A flapper costs less than a wax ring reset or a full replacement.
  • Fixture. Comfort-height, one-piece or wall-hung models vary.
  • Floor condition. Rot around the flange adds work.
  • Parts. Supply lines and valves are often replaced at the same time.
  • Hour. After-hours calls have their own rate.

Wobbles, leaks at the base and wax rings

A toilet that rocks or leaks at the floor usually has a failed wax ring or loose closet bolts. Water at the base can soak the subfloor over time, so do not ignore it. We reset the toilet with a new seal, check the flange and test for leaks.

If the flange is cracked or the floor is soft, we explain the repair before putting the toilet back.

Standards for toilet work

Toilet repair and replacement fall under the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), including flange height, venting and the seal at the floor. A wobbling toilet is a seal problem, and we repair it to those requirements.
