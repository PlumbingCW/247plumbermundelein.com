Tub Faucet Leak Repair: Mundelein, IL Homeowner Guide

A tub faucet that drips from the spout or leaks behind the wall wastes water and can damage drywall. Most causes are simple wear parts.

Call (847) 281-8626

By the Mundelein 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026. Updated November 12, 2026.

Where the leak shows up

Water from the tub spout when the faucet is off points to a worn cartridge or valve seat. Water from the shower head when the tub spout is on may be a diverter issue. Water around the handle means an O-ring or packing nut. A stain on the ceiling below suggests the leak is inside the wall.

Locating the drip tells the plumber which part to bring.

Cartridge and valve types

Single-handle tub valves use a cartridge. Two-handle tub valves use stems with washers or cartridges. Older houses may have compression stems that wear out with use. Newer houses use pressure-balancing cartridges that last longer but are specific to a brand.

Knowing the brand is half the battle. Look for a name on the handle, the trim plate or inside the cartridge.

Access is the main challenge

Many tubs have no access panel. A plumber may need to cut an access hole from the back side in an adjacent closet or room, or work through the tile. A good plan sets the hole in a place that is easy to patch.

If you have a spare tile or know the tile model, tell the plumber. Matching old tile is harder than most people expect.

What we do on the job

We shut off the water, remove the handle and trim, check the cartridge and replace worn parts. We test for leaks at the spout, handle and behind the wall, and we restore the trim. If the valve body itself is corroded, we explain the options and the extra work.

In a few cases the best answer is to replace the valve entirely, which requires opening the wall.

Shower and tub combinations

In a combined tub and shower, one valve controls both outlets and a diverter sends water to the tub spout or shower head. When the diverter wears out, water leaks from the spout while the shower runs. Replacing the spout or the diverter cartridge fixes it.

Some spouts attach with a set screw under the lip and others thread onto a pipe nipple. Do not twist a spout that is stuck, as it can damage the pipe inside the wall.

Caulk is not a plumbing repair

Caulk around the tub and tile prevents water from reaching the wall, but it does not stop a leak from the faucet itself. If the caulk fails, water enters the wall and may rot the framing. Check it once a year and renew it when it cracks.

A caulk repair can hide the true source of a leak, so find the source first.

What it costs to wait

A slow leak behind a tub can run for months and ruin drywall, insulation and floor framing before it shows. The repair is often small at first. Delay increases the damage, the drying time and the final bill.

If you see a stain or smell mold near the tub, call. A moisture meter helps us tell whether the wall is wet.

Choosing replacement parts

Use parts made for your valve brand and model. A universal cartridge may fit loosely and drip again within weeks. Bring the old cartridge or a clear photo to the supply house, or let the plumber bring the right match. If the trim is discontinued, a full valve replacement may be cleaner.

Ask for a written note of the brand and model for your records.

Signs the valve body is failing

If the handle is hard to turn, the trim wobbles or water leaks from the wall after a new cartridge goes in, the valve body may be corroded or cracked. That is a bigger job, since the old valve must be cut out and a new one soldered or crimped into place. It is still much cheaper to do it before the wall rots.

We tell you when a cartridge will do and when the valve itself has to go.

Codes and standards

Tub and shower valves are covered by the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), which sets requirements for temperature and pressure control.

Mundelein notes

Mundelein water arrives at about 8 grains per gallon per the Central Lake County Joint Action Water Agency. That moderate hardness is mild, but scale still collects on shower valves and cartridges over many years. Older houses near downtown may also have galvanized supply lines that leave rust-colored water at the tub.

What to check before you call

  • Whether the leak is from the spout, handle or shower head.
  • Whether hot, cold or both feel affected.
  • If there are stains on any ceiling or wall nearby.
  • The brand name on the handle.
  • Whether you have tile samples left from the install.

Emergency or not

A slow drip is not an emergency. Water spraying inside a wall is. If you cannot stop the water at the fixture, close the main and call. Water inside a wall can ruin drywall quickly.

Prevention

Do not over-tighten handles. Run both hot and cold on occasion to prevent seats from sticking. If the water has a strong mineral build-up, wipe the trim and spout with vinegar.
