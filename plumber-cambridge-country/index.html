A mix of houses and attached homes

Cambridge Country is a subdivision of roughly 650 homes just west of Midlothian Road and south of Hawley Street. Cambridge Homes built it between 1978 and 1997, and listing data describes a mix of single-family houses, townhomes and duplexes.

That mix matters for plumbing. In a detached house, a leak mostly affects your own floor. In an attached unit, water can reach a neighbor, so we focus on finding the source quickly and showing you where your shutoffs are.

Things attached-home owners should know

These points apply to townhomes and duplexes more than to detached houses.

Find your shutoff

Know where the main valve and the water heater valve are before a leak starts. We can label them during any visit.

Check your association rules

Some associations set rules on who can work in shared areas. Ask your board before opening a shared wall.

Report shared-wall leaks fast

Water that appears on a neighbor's ceiling can come from your unit. Call early and take photos.

Ask about insurance

Your policy and the association's policy may cover different parts. Keep paperwork from the repair.

Why water heaters come up here

Homes in this subdivision were completed between 1978 and 1997, so many original water heaters have likely been replaced by now. A tank that is 10 or more years old is worth checking even if it still works, because sediment builds up and a failing tank can leak into a finished space.

In a townhome the heater often sits in a closet or utility room next to living space. A drain pan and a visible shutoff are inexpensive protection, and we can add both when we replace the unit.

Local note

Homebuyer data lists Cambridge Country in Mundelein, Lake County, and the subdivision sits near the Hawley Street corridor. For water supply and hardness, check the utility on your bill. Mundelein village customers receive Lake Michigan water at about 8 grains per gallon, and Mundelein Public Works at 847-949-3270 can answer street-side water and sewer questions. Fixture and heater replacements here are done to the Illinois Plumbing Code, Title 77 Part 890.
