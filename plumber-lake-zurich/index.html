A Lake Zurich emergency begins with the water you have

Lake Zurich is a village with its own well supply. The village says its water comes from deep underground aquifers, and that ion-exchange treatment installed between 2003 and 2009 lowered hardness from 14 to 18 grains per gallon down to 6 to 8. That makes it softer than many well towns, and it affects what we find in a water heater or a faucet.

The Central Lake County Joint Action Water Agency lists Lake Zurich as coming by December 1, 2028. Until the connection arrives, expect well-water behavior such as scale at hot spots and softeners set for lower hardness. Our emergency plumber Lake Zurich IL line is answered 24/7.

Softeners and the village guidance

The village tells residents with a softener to set its regeneration rate for 6 to 8 grains per gallon and says residents can put a softener in bypass to try the current water. If you bought a unit years ago when hardness was higher, it may be overworking. We can adjust or service it, see water softener and filtration, and a hardness test at the tap is the first step.

Lake homes and what they ask of plumbing

Many Lake Zurich homes sit near water. The lake and its connected low areas raise groundwater after storms, which tests sump pumps and floor drains. Older lake cottages converted to year-round homes sometimes carry mixed plumbing from several decades, with cast iron, copper and plastic in the same house.

Houses on septic are a different matter. If your home is not on the village sewer, the Lake County Health Department permits septic systems, and we send you there for system questions. We handle the plumbing inside the house up to the tank.

Hardness and what to expect from fixtures

Because the village reduced hardness with ion-exchange treatment, scale builds more slowly than in untreated well towns. Even so, aerators, showerheads and water heater elements collect mineral over time. A yearly heater flush and a vinegar soak for aerators keep both in good shape.

If your softener is older than the treatment upgrade, have it checked so it is not wasting salt and water.

Calling from a lake-area home

Houses near the water often have lower basements and higher water tables, so a failed sump pump matters more than it does uphill. Test the pump twice a year and consider a battery backup. If water reaches the floor, close the main valve, stay out of standing water near outlets and call.

We answer 24/7, and the same crew handles the pump, the discharge line and the floor drain.

Rules that apply in Lake Zurich

Work on house plumbing follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code). Well, septic and pressure tank questions also involve the Lake County Health Department, which permits private systems.
