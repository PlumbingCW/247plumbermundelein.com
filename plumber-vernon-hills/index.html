Where Vernon Hills water comes from

Lake County Public Works reports that Vernon Hills water is pumped from Lake Michigan and treated at the Paul M. Neal Water Treatment Facility in Lake Bluff. The county buys it from the Central Lake County Joint Action Water Agency. The Lake County Public Works number printed in that report is (847) 377-7500.

That matters for plumbing in two ways. Lake Michigan water is moderately hard, so heaters and tankless units scale slowly. And a pressure drop or discolored water that hits the whole street is a county utility question first, not a pipe question inside your house.

Newer homes, newer problems

Vernon Hills is known for newer subdivisions and a large retail core around Route 60 and Route 21. Newer houses do not fail the way a 1950s house does. We see PEX fitting leaks, water heaters with sediment, builder-grade faucets and disposal jams more than corroded galvanized lines.

Because the pipe is newer, the question after a leak is usually where it started and whether the same fitting type was used elsewhere. Our leak detection service pinpoints the spot, and a pressure test of the manifold tells us if other fittings are at risk.

Seasonal pressure on Vernon Hills plumbing

Winter splits garage and exterior-wall lines. Spring thaw tests sump pumps. Summer storms push water through floor drains, and fall is when many sellers and buyers order inspections. Each season brings different calls to the same dispatcher.

If your basement is wet after a storm, start with sump pump repair before you call anyone about waterproofing.

Condos, townhomes and shared walls

In attached homes a leak can reach a neighbor within minutes. Know where your unit's shutoff is, and tell the association contact if water crosses a wall. We document the source in writing so insurance and the association can sort out responsibility.

For a failed water heater in a unit, ask the association about approved replacement locations and venting before work starts.

Commercial neighbors along Route 60

Restaurants, shops and offices have their own needs, such as grease lines, restroom fixtures and floor drains. After-hours calls are common because many failures show up at closing time. Our dispatcher routes commercial calls to a plumber who works on those systems.

Rules that apply in Vernon Hills

Plumbing in Vernon Hills follows the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code), with permits handled locally. Commercial properties may face added requirements for grease and backflow protection.
