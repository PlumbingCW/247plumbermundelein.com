Plumbing installation in Mundelein, IL for remodels and additions

Plumbing installation is the work of running new supply and drain lines and connecting fixtures. It includes basement bathrooms, kitchen remodels, laundry moves, new sinks and showers, and water lines to outdoor spigots.

We handle the plumbing from layout to final test, coordinating with your contractor when there is one. A licensed plumber walks the job, marks fixture locations and explains what the walls and floors need to allow.

Jobs we install

  • Basement bathrooms, including ejector pumps where the drain sits below the sewer.
  • Kitchen sinks, dishwashers, disposals and pot fillers.
  • Showers, tubs and body-spray valves.
  • Laundry hookups and utility sinks.
  • Outdoor hose bibs and frost-free spigots.
  • Water softener and filter connections.

New plumbing generally involves permit and inspection steps, and requirements differ between villages. Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health, and you can verify any plumber's license on the state lookup tool before you hire.

What affects installation pricing

Installations are quoted in writing before work begins.

  • Scope. One fixture is a very different job than a whole bathroom.
  • Access. Open studs cost less than finished walls.
  • Drain location. Sewer depth and distance affect the layout.
  • Fixture grade. Fixtures you choose change the total.
  • Schedule. Working around your contractor may mean evenings or weekends.

Local conditions that affect new plumbing

Homes near Diamond Lake and Countryside Lake can sit at a lower elevation than the street sewer, which affects basement bath plans. A sewage ejector pump is common in that case. We explain the pump, its alarm and the maintenance it needs. For older homes near downtown Mundelein, tie-ins may need new shutoffs or a section of repiping.

If your project includes a new hot water source, the water heater installation page covers sizing and venting.

Planning the rough-in

New fixtures begin with a plan for supply, drain and vent. The plumber measures distances, checks slope and decides where each line will run before any wall is closed. Moving a toilet or a shower drain can mean cutting concrete or reworking a joist, so it is worth settling the layout before tile is ordered.

Bring fixture specs to the planning visit. The rough-in dimensions of a toilet, a vanity or a shower valve decide where pipe must stop.

Permits, inspections and finish work

Most installations that add or move pipe need a permit, and some need a rough-in inspection before walls close. The Mundelein building office at 847-949-3283 can tell you what applies at your address. We explain which inspections your job needs and schedule the work around them.

After inspection comes the finish: setting fixtures, testing for leaks and running every drain. We test each connection under pressure before the job is called complete.

Permits and standards for new work

New fixtures, drains and supply lines are covered by the Illinois Plumbing Code, the state standard (Title 77, Part 890 of the Illinois Administrative Code). A rough-in inspection before walls close is common. The Mundelein building office at 847-949-3283 can tell you what applies to your project.
