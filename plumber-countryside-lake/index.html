Plumbing on a large wooded lot

Countryside Lake is a private lake community of about 370 residences on the west side of Mundelein. The association describes homesites of about 0.9 acres or larger in a heavily wooded setting. That lot size shapes the plumbing more than anything else here.

A bigger lot usually means a longer run between the house and the street or the septic field. Longer runs give roots more time to find a joint, and a slow drain on a long line can hide a problem for months before it backs up.

What we check at Countryside Lake homes

Our first visit follows the layout of the lot, not a standard checklist.

  • Sewer or septic connection. We ask which system serves the house before we pick a tool.
  • Root intrusion on long lateral lines, confirmed with a camera instead of guessed.
  • Sump pump and discharge path, especially for houses close to the shoreline.
  • Water heater age, venting and drain pan condition in finished basements.
  • Outdoor spigots and buried supply lines that cross wooded ground.

If a drain has slowed on one fixture only, the cause is usually local to that fixture. If every drain is sluggish, the main line is the better place to look.

Good to know before you call

The Countryside Lake Association states that the community lies in unincorporated Mundelein and Hawthorn Woods, within Fremont Township in Lake County. Because some Lake County homes outside village limits use private wells and septic systems, confirm which system your address has. Lake County Environmental Health publishes the permit rules for wells and septic work.
